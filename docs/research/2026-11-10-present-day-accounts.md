# Present-day crossing accounts

Date: 2026-11-10. Source: a research pass in another Claude session that collected 25 present-day accounts from eight countries, almost all from pages it opened itself. Yury pasted its summary into this session. The region researchers came back later the same day, and their findings are in "How the crossing works" below.

This note keeps mechanisms only. Most accounts are about named living people, several of them in custody, so no names, places or trip-plus-charge combinations are kept here, and none should go into the game.

## TL;DR

- In the real accounts the desk is rarely where it ends. The consequence comes later, somewhere else, or through the family. The game settles every ending at the desk today.
- This supports carry-over (rwf.2), a consequence after you get home (rwf.10) and the exit desk in Departure (rwf.3) more strongly than anything found before.
- Preparation in these accounts often fails. Showing that is the honest version, and it keeps the game from reading as a guide.

## Where the consequence lands

- At home, days later. A traveller crossed with a phone they had cleaned and was taken outside their parents' flat. Another crossed with no checks and was arrested weeks later from a bank statement.
- On the way out. A traveller came in to see an ailing parent without trouble, and both passports were taken at the exit desk. An officer in another case: "How can I let him go if the paperwork has come to me?"
- As a wait with no end date. The passport is kept, with "free to go - for now", then six months of weekly visits to an office.
- Through the family. An unknown man phones a sibling. A parent is called within hours and told to "educate your daughter". A mother begs her son never to call again, so silence is the protection.

## Smaller beats and where they fit

- Phone: likes from years ago found on a phone the owner thought was cleaned. "Why did you delete your chats?" as the first question. An officer reading an unread message from the traveller's mother.
- Contact: a sibling's number nobody thought to delete.
- Document: an arrivals form asking for addresses, income and countries visited. An ID renewal as the errand that forces the trip.
- Waiting room: a basement corridor with chairs while the night bus waits six hours. An officer scrolls your profile, then walks you out because you don't know the way.
- Officer: "She entered the data and her face changed."
- Preparation: relatives saying they'd feel safer if you didn't come. Checking whether your name is on a list before travelling.
- Not going at all: a funeral watched on a phone propped by the coffin, with relatives abroad lighting a candle beside their screens. This would be a fifth ending. Not filed on 2026-11-10 (Yury chose not to).
- Turned back in sight of home: refused entry and not allowed to say goodbye to an 86-year-old mother.

## What not to do

- Don't use a recognisable trip plus charge from a real account, because the people are alive and several are in custody.
- Don't map the game one-to-one onto Russia and Ukraine (a donation plus a war question). Blend in the ID renewal, faith and banned-book cases.

## Not found

- An officer quietly advising a traveller.
- A neighbour-informer story tied to a visit.
- An account of how someone behaved on their next trip.
- First-person accounts from Eritrea, Turkey, Tajikistan or Turkmenistan.

## What was filed from this

- rwf.10 Consequence after home: a recorded claim that a document disproves can fire a beat days after you get home, turning homeInTime into homeAtCost, and the beat stays in the carried file.
- rwf.3 Departure: the exit desk can hold your passport when the carried file has a disproved claim, ending turnedBack with "free to go for now".
- Not filed: the fifth "not going" ending and a content bead for the smaller beats.

## How the crossing works (12 countries)

From the region researchers' summary, pasted later on 2026-11-10. Twelve countries share one shape in eight stages. The game models three of them (the booth, the second office, customs).

1. Before travel - a file already exists (declared foreign status, registries, a tax or apology paper).
2. Before boarding - the airline screens the passenger list and can refuse you.
3. The booth - a silent flag, the passport kept, "wait, you'll be called".
4. The second office - a different service, a typed questionnaire you never see, the phone leaves the room.
5. Customs - X-ray, cash arithmetic, a seizure paper.
6. Release - no paper, or a slip with an office and a deadline.
7. In the country - registration, a visit to the host, summonses.
8. The exit desk - a ban discovered only here.

Across regions: no reason is given, agencies overrule each other, the home passport is the only one that counts, and arrest usually comes later.

## Material worth using

- After the stamp: a slip with a 10-day deadline, the knock at the parents' flat, the passport taken on the way out.
- Before the queue: an "am I clear?" inquiry that may be worthless, relatives saying don't come, not going at all.
- Ways a story comes apart: a booking shorter than the stated stay, a host whose account differs, a bank notification, a sibling's number never deleted, old likes on a phone thought cleaned.
- Rules with history: a relative asked the same question, hours as evidence, someone who stays behind as your guarantee, release only when a named contact answers.
- Warmth: an officer who phones your mother about potatoes, an officer whose father took the same drugs, a woman on the bus handing out exactly the allowance, interviews that shrink for regulars.
- Customs: declaring costs nothing, the fine is for hiding.
- Small details: two corners snipped off a passport, a whole bus waiting, "the system is down", being told to sleep in a hotel and not at your mother's.

Also avoid: asylum claims, escorted removal, real agency names, a stock image of one nationality.

## How solid this is

- Well sourced: Russia, Iran, China, Eritrea, Cuba, and most present-day stories.
- Thin: Belarus, Turkey, Egypt, the contrast countries, officer kindness, reunion scenes, several historical details.
- Not checked: the researchers' sources were not opened in this session, and nobody has checked whether the engine can express each new rule.
