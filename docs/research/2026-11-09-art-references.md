# Purpose of Visit - art references

Researched 2026-11-09. Links were checked by fetching them unless marked "(not fetched)". I did not look at screenshots myself (the tools return text only), so the one-line "look" notes come from store/press descriptions and general knowledge of the games, not from fresh viewing.

## TL;DR

- Strongest direction: low-colour pixel scenes (8-colour base palette, one amber light source per scene), papers drawn bigger and crisper than the room, and the official's face kept half in shadow.
- Base palette: Dreamscape8 (Kerrie Lake) - it already holds muted green-greys, a cream, an amber (#caa05a) and a dusty red (#8b4049) that can stay reserved for serious consequences.
- Reference 1: NORCO - painterly pixel interiors and night exteriors with one warm practical light against cool greens.
- Reference 2: Citizen Sleeper - portrait-friendly layering of a pixel scene under clean cream text panels, tension carried by type and pacing, not gore.
- Reference 3: Death and Taxes - a desk of physical paper objects as the whole interface, decisions made by handling documents.

## Games

Each entry: look - one lesson - what not to copy - link.

1. **Reigns** (Nerial / Devolver). Flat vector card on a portrait screen, one character per card, 2-3 colours. Lesson: one question card at a time, centred, with the answer choice shown as the card tilting toward a side, so the card stays the hero of a 390px-wide screen. Don't copy: the swipe-left/right binary, the four resource meters, the flat vector faces. https://devolverdigital.com/games/reigns
2. **Death and Taxes** (Placeholder Gameworks). Top-down office desk with papers, a lamp and small props you physically move. Lesson: documents as tactile objects with drop shadows and slight rotation, so "showing a paper" feels like a hand action. Don't copy: the Grim Reaper office comedy, the desk layout, the fate-file design. https://store.steampowered.com/app/1166290/
3. **Beholder** (Warm Lamp Games / Alawar). Cut-away apartment block in grey-brown, characters as dark silhouettes with only eyes visible. Lesson: hide the official's face mostly in shadow (silhouette + lit hands/papers), because it reads as authority without a scary face. Don't copy: the doll-house cross-section, the shadow-people character design. https://store.steampowered.com/app/475550/Beholder/
4. **Citizen Sleeper** (Jump Over The Age / Fellow Traveller). Painted characters and station art under very clean UI type panels, muted with neon accents. Lesson: keep the art a calm backdrop and let a cream text panel carry the scene; one accent colour signals what needs attention (here: amber). Don't copy: the dice-slot mechanics, the cyberpunk palette, their character portraits. https://store.steampowered.com/app/1578650/
5. **Disco Elysium** (ZA/UM). Oil-painted, desaturated greens, browns and greys, with grim bureaucratic interiors. Lesson: desaturate the whole frame and allow saturation only where the eye should go (a stamp, a lamp, a pill box). Don't copy: the painterly portraits, the skill-voice UI, the isometric camera. https://discoelysium.com/
6. **Kentucky Route Zero** (Cardboard Computer). Flat low-poly shapes, big empty dark spaces, theatrical spotlights. Lesson: negative space and one pool of light make a quiet, uneasy room; the dark around the counter is part of the composition. Don't copy: the stage-play camera, the flat-shaded 3D look. https://kentuckyroutezero.com/
7. **Signalis** (rose-engine). Low-res PS1-style 3D plus crisp pixel UI, red and grey, institutional corridors. Lesson: hard-edged, strictly gridded document/UI chrome next to soft low-res scenes makes the paperwork feel official. Don't copy: the horror tone, red-dominant palette, its specific UI frames. https://rose-engine.org/signalis/ (not fetched; listed as official site by two game databases) and https://store.steampowered.com/app/1262350/ (not fetched)
8. **Return of the Obra Dinn** (Lucas Pope / 3909). 1-bit dithered 3D. Lesson: a hard palette limit (even 2 colours) gives a whole game one consistent look; useful as a discipline when PixelLab output drifts. Don't copy: the 1-bit look itself or anything else by the same author (he also made Papers, Please). https://obradinn.com/
9. **Unpacking** (Witch Beam). Bright pixel-art rooms told only through objects. Lesson: the medicine box, the father's letter and the bus ticket should each be readable at small size and tell story on their own, with no text. Don't copy: the bright cosy palette, the isometric room framing. Link: Wikipedia only, no official page found - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unpacking_(video_game)
10. **NORCO** (Geography of Robots / Raw Fury). "Painterly and cinematic pixel art" (publisher's words) of Louisiana refineries, swamps and run-down interiors. Lesson: one warm practical light (lamp, sodium streetlight) against cool green-grey shadows, with dithered falloff, is exactly the amber-on-muted-green rule. Don't copy: its scenes, characters, or the side-on adventure framing. https://rawfury.com/games/norco/ (has a press kit link to Google Drive)
11. **Eastward** (Pixpil / Chucklefish). Dense pixel art with strong coloured lighting and soft glow. Lesson: light is painted as its own layer (a glow shape + one brighter rim on objects), not as shading on every pixel. Don't copy: the saturated anime palette, characters, top-down RPG framing. https://store.steampowered.com/app/977880/Eastward/
12. **Through the Darkest of Times** (Paintbucket Games). Expressionist, near-monochrome illustration with a single red accent, set in 1930s Berlin. Lesson: one reserved accent colour with a fixed meaning; here red = confirmed serious consequence, never decoration. Don't copy: the woodcut-style figures, the historical setting. https://paintbucket.de/en/ttdot

Also checked, weaker fit for art lessons:
- Road 96 (DigixArt) - border crossing as a whole-game premise, 3D stylised; useful for tone, not pixel look. Official site digixart.com (not fetched).
- Mind Scanners (The Outer Zone) - retro-futurist checkpoint/clinic interviews; press kit with 6 screenshots: https://www.braveatnight.co.uk/mindscannerspresskit
- Contraband Police (Crazy Rocks) - realistic 3D Eastern-bloc border post; good for props, nothing for pixel style. https://store.steampowered.com/app/756800/
- Pentiment (Obsidian) - manuscript look, typography as character. https://store.steampowered.com/app/1205520/
- Orwell (Osmotic Studios) - documents as evidence, desktop UI. https://www.osmoticstudios.com/about/
- Cultist Simulator (Weather Factory) - cards as physical tabletop objects. https://weatherfactory.biz/cultist-simulator/
- Not Tonight (PanicBarn) - door-check game in pixel art; too close to the Papers, Please formula, so listed only as something to avoid echoing. https://store.steampowered.com/app/733790/ (not fetched)

## Palettes

Hex values copied from each Lospec page.

1. **Dreamscape8** by Kerrie Lake (8). https://lospec.com/palette-list/dreamscape8
   `#c9cca1 #caa05a #ae6a47 #8b4049 #543344 #515262 #63787d #8ea091`
   Why: best single fit. Cream (#c9cca1) for paper, amber (#caa05a) for light/attention, green-greys (#63787d #8ea091) for walls, dusty red (#8b4049) to hold back for consequences. Low saturation by design.
2. **Ammo-8** by rsvp asap (8). https://lospec.com/palette-list/ammo-8
   `#040c06 #112318 #1e3a29 #305d42 #4d8061 #89a257 #bedc7f #eeffcc`
   Why: a clean dark-green ramp for the frame, UI chrome and night scenes; pair the darks with Dreamscape8's amber.
3. **Lost Century** by SurrealEmber (16). https://lospec.com/palette-list/lost-century
   `#d1b187 #c77b58 #ae5d40 #79444a #4b3d44 #ba9158 #927441 #4d4539 #77743b #b3a555 #d2c9a5 #8caba1 #4b726e #574852 #847875 #ab9b8e`
   Why: wider earthy set (aged paper #d2c9a5, olive, teal-grey #4b726e, brass #ba9158) for detailed props like stamps, forms, wooden counters.
4. **Fading 16** by SurrealEmber (16). https://lospec.com/palette-list/fading-16
   `#ddcf99 #cca87b #b97a60 #9c524e #774251 #4b3d44 #4e5463 #5b7d73 #8e9f7d #645355 #8c7c79 #a99c8d #7d7b62 #aaa25d #846d59 #a88a5e`
   Why: faded, worn-out feel that suits old institutional interiors; has cream, sage greens and a warm amber-tan.
5. **The Fated Eight** by Digi (8). https://lospec.com/palette-list/the-fated-eight
   `#4c2a1b #513e30 #664a4d #6b5f61 #807f7f #94a09e #99b5b3 #b0cad9`
   Why: author says it is for "moody, foggy scenes"; cold greys with warm brown darks. Good for the rainy bus window, add one amber from Dreamscape8 for the hospital lights.
6. **Vinik24** by Vinik (24). https://lospec.com/palette-list/vinik24
   `#000000 #6f6776 #9a9a97 #c5ccb8 #8b5580 #c38890 #a593a5 #666092 #9a4f50 #c28d75 #7ca1c0 #416aa3 #8d6268 #be955c #68aca9 #387080 #6e6962 #93a167 #6eaa78 #557064 #9d9f7f #7e9e99 #5d6872 #433455`
   Why: soft muted ramps that share shadows and highlights, so mixed scenes stay coherent; use as the "big" fallback if 8 colours is too tight.

Suggested use: Dreamscape8 as the scene palette, Ammo-8 darks for the frame, and feed the hex list straight into PixelLab's palette field.

## PixelLab prompts

What the current docs say (pixellab.ai/docs, read 2026-11-09):
- **Create medium-extra large image (pixflux)**: min area 32x32, max area 200x200 (free), 320x320 (tier 1), 400x400 (tier 2+). Options: description, negative description, camera view, direction, outline, shading, details, isometric, guidance weight, Target Palette, init image, remove background, seed. No style image on this tool. Docs suggest about 16:9 for scenery. https://www.pixellab.ai/docs/tools/create-image-flux
- **Create S-XL image (Pro)**: 16x16 up to about a quarter megapixel; square max 512x512, widest 792x336 (21:9), portrait sizes are the transposes. Up to 4 reference images plus one **style image** that sets palette, outline, detail and shading (Style Options choose which). **Color palette (beta)**: presets, a Lospec link, a palette image or hex list, plus "Max colours"; a set palette overrides the style image's colours. https://www.pixellab.ai/docs/tools/create-sl-image-pro
- **Create Images from Style References (Pro)** (consistent style): several style images, max 512x512, needs tier 1; meant for props/characters that match each other. https://www.pixellab.ai/docs/tools/consistent-style
- **Camera** options: None, Low top-down (~20 degrees), High top-down (~35 degrees), Side. Docs say these are weak and an init image or wording works better. There is no "first-person over the shoulder" view, so describe it in words and/or use an init image (for example a current SVG scene exported to PNG). https://www.pixellab.ai/docs/options/camera
- **Color**: "Limit colors used" (weak), "Force colors" (strict match to Target Palette), quantization to a palette or colour count. https://www.pixellab.ai/docs/options/color
- Also useful: Reduce colors, Image to pixel art, Inpaint, Edit image.

Suggested workflow: generate scenes with S-XL Pro at a portrait size near 9:19.5 (for example 192x416 and let it snap to the nearest ratio), set Color palette to the Dreamscape8 Lospec link with max 8-10 colours, then scale x2 nearest-neighbour for the 390x844 screen. Use the first good scene as the style image for the other two. Generate the papers and the medicine box separately with the consistent-style tool, transparent background, so they stay interactive objects in the UI.

## What not to do

- Don't recreate Papers, Please. That means no booth-window-plus-desk split screen, no drag-and-drop document desk layout, no stamp-approve/deny loop visuals, no rulebook UI, no grey-brown 1980s palette with red stamps, no "Glory to Arstotzka"-style state iconography. It's the obvious comparison, so it is named here only as the thing to avoid copying. Same caution for anything else by Lucas Pope.
- Don't copy any listed game's characters, layouts, frames or palettes outright; take the lesson, not the look.
- Don't use red as decoration or for "suspicion". Amber marks attention; red only for a confirmed serious consequence.
- Don't let PixelLab add readable text, real flags, real emblems or real-country insignia, because Veyra and the Union are fictional and invented text in AI output looks wrong.
- Don't mix palettes per scene. Force the palette in PixelLab so three generated scenes still look like one game.
- Don't scale generated images by non-integer factors on 390px wide screens, because pixels will blur; scale x2 or x3 nearest-neighbour and crop.
- Don't show violence, weapons drawn or uniformed menace; tension comes from light, waiting and paper.
